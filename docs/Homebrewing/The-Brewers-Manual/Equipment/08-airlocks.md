# Airlocks

:::caution Under construction
This page covers the essentials. Additional sections — cold crash suck-back detail, retirement criteria, and the grommet/stem interface — are in preparation.
:::

An airlock is a one-way valve fitted to the fermenter lid. It allows CO₂ produced during fermentation to escape while preventing air, wild yeast, and bacteria from entering the fermenter. It is one of the simplest components in the setup and one of the most important.

---

## Types

Two designs are in common use. Both work.

**3-piece airlock** — a cylindrical body with a removable cap and an inner float. The float rises on CO₂ pressure and lets gas escape around the rim, then drops back to seal when pressure equalises. Easy to disassemble and clean thoroughly. Recommended.

**S-type (double-bubble) airlock** — a single moulded S-curve body. Gas bubbles through the liquid fill in both chambers. Cannot be fully disassembled — cleaning is limited to flushing. Adequate for most use but the 3-piece is easier to maintain.

---

## Materials

Airlocks are typically made from **PP** or **SAN** (styrene-acrylonitrile). Both are A-rated for all brewing chemicals at ambient temperature.

Some airlocks — particularly compact designs — may be made from **GPPS** (general-purpose polystyrene). GPPS is chemically weaker than SAN and should not be soaked in concentrated ABNS or alkaline cleaners for extended periods. If the material of your airlock is not stated, treat it as GPPS and limit soak time to 30 minutes maximum. See the [PS/SAN materials page](../05-materials-register/05-ps-san.md) for the full compatibility analysis.

:::tip How to tell SAN from GPPS
SAN is typically crystal-clear with a faint blue tint. GPPS is clear but more brittle and has a slightly duller appearance. When in doubt, treat as GPPS.
:::

## Fill liquid

The liquid in the airlock U-section creates the seal. The choice of fill liquid matters.

**Working-dilution ABNS (StarSan, StellarSan)** — the recommended fill liquid. Already sanitised, compatible with all airlock materials, and harmless if suck-back occurs during cold crashing. Use the same solution you mixed for brew day sanitisation.

**Cooled boiled water** — acceptable if no ABNS is available. Plain tap water carries a small contamination risk if suck-back occurs; boiled and cooled water eliminates this.

**Do not use undiluted DES (ethanol)** — at 70–80% concentration, suck-back delivers a slug of alcohol directly into the fermenter headspace, which can damage or kill yeast at the liquid surface. DES is not suitable as an airlock fill liquid.

**Do not use plain tap water** — contamination risk from suck-back is low but non-zero. Cooled boiled water or ABNS is the better choice.

Fill to the indicated level on the airlock — typically the line marked on the body. Overfilling increases suck-back volume if negative pressure develops; underfilling reduces the seal.

---

## Installation

1. Sanitise the airlock by submerging in working-dilution ABNS for 30 seconds. Do not rinse.
2. Fill to the indicated level with fresh working-dilution ABNS.
3. Fit the airlock stem into the grommet in the fermenter lid. It should seat firmly with light hand pressure — push until resistance is felt, not until the grommet deforms.
4. Do not overtighten or force. The seal is made by the grommet compression against the stem, not by driving the airlock deep into the lid.

---

## Reading the airlock

**Bubbling** indicates CO₂ is escaping — fermentation is active, or the fermenter is still off-gassing after active fermentation has slowed.

**No bubbling** does not mean fermentation is complete. Common causes include: fermentation genuinely finished; temperature drop reducing CO₂ solubility and gas production; a small leak in the lid seal allowing CO₂ to escape another way. The only reliable way to confirm fermentation is complete is to measure gravity on two consecutive days and confirm it is stable. The airlock is a useful indicator, not a definitive one.

---

## Cleaning

**3-piece airlock:** disassemble completely after every brew. Soak all parts in warm alkaline cleaner (PBW or StellarClean) for 15 minutes. Rinse thoroughly. Inspect the float for residue in the joint around the rim.

**S-type airlock:** flush thoroughly with warm water immediately after use while residue is still wet. Soak assembled in alkaline cleaner and flush again. Full disassembly is not possible — if residue persists, replace rather than brew with a suspect airlock. Airlocks are inexpensive.

---

## Retirement

Replace an airlock if:
- The body is cracked or cloudy in a way that obscures the fill level
- The float (3-piece) no longer seats correctly or shows visible residue that cleaning cannot remove
- The stem is damaged or deformed in a way that compromises the grommet seal

Airlocks are consumables. A fresh airlock costs less than a ruined batch.
