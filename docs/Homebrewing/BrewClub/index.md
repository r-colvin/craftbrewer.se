# BrewClub

At some point in anything you love, you want to know more. You stop being a spectator and start asking *why*.

For beer, that question has a good answer: brew some. Not because it's cheaper than buying it — it probably isn't, once you're honest about your time. Not because the result is guaranteed to be better than what's on the shelf — it might not be, at first. But because the moment you brew your own beer, you start to understand what you're drinking in a way that no amount of reading or tasting can fully replicate. Why one beer is hazy and another is clear. Why a dry-hopped IPA smells the way it does straight from the fermenter. Why the same recipe brewed by two people can taste completely different.

It is a bit like learning an instrument. You don't have to play to enjoy music — but once you do, you hear it differently. You can be a spectator, and that's completely fine. But you won't understand it the way a participant does.

Brewing is hands-on, unhurried, and away from a screen. It is history, chemistry, and art. It requires attention to detail, rewards curiosity, and gives you something you made yourself to share with people you like. That's the point. If you're looking for a way to make cheap alcohol, this is not the right place — there are easier paths to that. But if you want to understand what you're drinking, get better at it over time, and share the results with friends, then you're in the right place.

## What is BrewClub?

BrewClub started as a way to get a group of friends brewing together — sharing a batch of wort, each choosing a different yeast or dry hop, then getting together to taste and compare. You learn more from one tasting session like that than from months of reading alone. Beer has been bringing people together since the beginning of human history, and there's something genuinely satisfying about adding your own to that tradition.

But you don't need a group to get started. Everything in BrewClub works just as well for a solo first brew. And if you pick up friends along the way — someone curious at a tasting, a neighbour who asks what's fermenting in your kitchen — you'll have everything you need to bring them in.

The format is whatever suits you. A formal session with a fixed time and place. A loose arrangement where everyone brews at home and compares notes online. Completely solo, with questions answered through the BrewClub community. There is no prescribed structure — the only encouragement is that if you do have friends doing the same thing, getting together at some point to share and discuss is where the most learning happens.

There is a BrewClub Discord for questions, discussion, and sharing between sessions. Access is through your club leader. ## What BrewClub is built around

**Low cost, low risk.** The equipment you need for Brew 1 is inexpensive. If you brew once and decide it's not for you, the sunk cost is minimal — most items have uses beyond brewing, and nothing you buy for Brew 1 will be wasted if you continue. Where we recommend buying something, it's because it will make your brewing meaningfully better *and* stay useful through your journey. We will never tell you to buy something big upfront. The right time to invest in a piece of equipment is when you've decided you want it — not before.

**Low time, low space.** Brew 1 fits into an evening. Brew day is around 30 minutes of active work. Fermentation looks after itself. Packaging is another short session. A kitchen counter is enough space. The hobby should fit around your life.

**Progressive learning.** Each brew builds on the last, adding one new skill. You can stop at any point — there is no obligation to continue, and each stage is genuinely complete in itself. But if you finish all four brews, you will have a solid foundation: enough to read the books, follow online discussions, and understand what's being talked about. Enough to go anywhere the hobby takes you.

**The why, not just the what.** BrewClub always explains the reasoning behind each step. When we recommend one approach over another, we say why. When we deviate from kit instructions, we explain the thinking. The goal is that you understand your brew well enough to make your own decisions — not just follow a checklist.

## The brew sequence

BrewClub is built around four brews. Each one adds something new. You can stop at any station — there is no pressure to continue, and Brew 1 is the only one that's truly mandatory.

**Brew 1 — Fresh wort kit or extract pouch kit**
No boiling, no mashing. You take a professionally made wort, pitch your yeast, ferment, and package. The focus is entirely on the cold side: sanitising, fermentation, packaging. The highest chance of success, the lowest barrier to entry.

*In a club:* one fresh wort kit splits across four or five people. Everyone pitches a different yeast, or chooses a different dry hop, or ferments at a different temperature. The tasting session is the point — comparing what each variable did to the same starting wort.

*Solo:* an extract pouch kit splits into two smaller batches. Brew one now, store the other half for your next brew.

**Brew 2 — Cider, or an adjuncted beer**
Cider introduces yeast nutrient and the concept of adjuncts — dry hops, spices, fruit additions, back-sweetening. If you brewed beer for Brew 1, Brew 2 is the moment to try cider, and vice versa. The two are interchangeable and both are repeatable. There is no wrong order.

**Brew 3 — Stove-top extract with partial mash**
First time making your own wort. Steeping grain, dissolving extract, a short boil with hop additions. A pot, a grain bag, and a thermometer are the only new equipment required. This is where you start to understand what malt and hops actually contribute.

**Brew 4 — Stove-top all-grain**
Full control over the recipe from grain to glass. Mashing, sparging, boiling, hopping — everything. No new equipment beyond Brew 3. This is where the hobby opens up completely.

## Pick your track

At each stage, BrewClub offers options rather than a single prescription. The right choice depends on your equipment, your group, and what you want to drink.

**Packaging:** bottles (carbonation drops into PET or glass) or a mini keg (Oxebar 4L with a picnic tap). Both are covered throughout. Neither is wrong for a first brew.

**Carbonation:** natural (carbonation drops or priming sugar, conditioned at room temperature) or force carbonation (CO₂ gas, faster and more predictable). Natural carbonation needs no gas equipment; force carbonation gives more consistent results.

**Fermentation temperature:** standard ale yeast works well between 18–22°C. Kveik strains ferment reliably at 20–40°C — useful if your house runs warm or you're brewing in summer. The fermentation section walks you through the decision.

You don't need to make all of these choices before you start. Each section of BrewClub introduces the options at the point where they become relevant.

## What this guide is — and isn't

BrewClub is not a replacement for the established brewing texts. John Palmer's *How to Brew*, Mitch Steele's *IPA*, Graham Wheeler's books — these are excellent, and if you finish BrewClub and want to go deeper, they are exactly where to go. BrewClub is the on-ramp: a free, structured, low-risk path that gets you brewing quickly and gives you the foundation to engage with those resources on your own terms, without feeling lost.

If something in BrewClub is wrong, unclear, or missing — or if you want to add your own club's equipment recommendations and local sourcing — [open an issue or submit a pull request on GitHub](https://github.com/r-colvin/craftbrewer.se). That's how this guide gets better.
