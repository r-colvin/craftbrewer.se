# BrewClub

BrewClub started as a way to get a group of friends brewing together. The idea is simple: share the cost, share the learning, share the beer. By splitting a single batch of wort between four or five people — each choosing their own yeast, their own dry hop, their own packaging — everyone walks away with a unique beer from the same starting point. You learn more in one tasting session than you would from months of reading.

That said, you don't need a club to get started. If you're here on your own, everything in BrewClub works just as well for a solo first brew. And if you pick up friends along the way — someone curious at a tasting, a neighbour who asks what's fermenting in your kitchen — you'll have everything you need to bring them in.

## What BrewClub is built around

Four goals, in order of priority:

**Minimal cost.** The equipment you need for Brew 1 is inexpensive. If you brew once and decide it's not for you, nothing is wasted — it can be reused, passed on, or sold. Nothing in the starter kit is single-purpose.

**Minimal time.** Brew 1 is designed to fit into an evening. Brew day is around 30 minutes of active work. Fermentation looks after itself. Packaging is another short session. The hobby should fit around your life, not the other way around.

**Minimal space.** A kitchen counter is enough. A small fermenter, a mini keg, a spray bottle of sanitiser. No dedicated brew room, no large equipment.

**Learning that sticks.** Every brew introduces one new skill. By the time you've done all four, you understand what hops, malt, yeast, and process each contribute to the final beer — not because you memorised it, but because you tasted it.

## The brew sequence

BrewClub is built around four brews, each adding something new. You can stop at any point — there is no obligation to continue, and each stage is genuinely complete in itself.

**Brew 1 — Fresh wort kit or extract pouch kit.**
No boiling, no mashing. You take a professionally made wort, pitch your yeast, ferment, and package. The focus is entirely on the cold side: sanitising, fermentation, packaging. The highest chance of success, the lowest barrier to entry.

*In a club:* one fresh wort kit splits across four or five people. Everyone pitches a different yeast or chooses a different dry hop. The tasting session afterwards is the point — comparing what each variable did.

*Solo:* an extract pouch kit splits into two batches. Brew one now, store the other half for later.

**Brew 2 — Cider, or an adjuncted beer.**
Cider introduces yeast nutrient and the concept of adjuncts — dry hops, spices, back-sweetening. If you brewed beer first, this is the moment to try cider, and vice versa. Brew 1 and Brew 2 are interchangeable and repeatable; there is no wrong order.

**Brew 3 — Stove-top extract with partial mash.**
First time making your own wort. Steeping grain, dissolving extract, a short boil with hop additions. A pot, a grain bag, and a thermometer are the only new equipment required.

**Brew 4 — Stove-top all-grain.**
Full control over the recipe. Mashing, sparging, boiling, hopping — everything from grain to glass. No new equipment beyond Brew 3. This is where the hobby fully opens up.

## Pick your track

At each stage, BrewClub offers options rather than instructions. The right choice depends on your equipment, your group, and what you actually want to drink.

**Packaging:** bottles (carbonation drops into PET or glass) or a mini keg (Oxebar 4L with a picnic tap). Both are covered throughout. Neither is wrong for a first brew.

**Carbonation:** natural (carbonation drops or priming sugar, conditioned at room temperature) or force carbonation (CO₂ gas, faster and more controllable). Natural carbonation requires no gas equipment; force carbonation gives more consistent results.

**Fermentation temperature:** standard ale yeast works well between 18–22°C. Kveik strains ferment reliably at 20–40°C — useful if your house runs warm or you're brewing in summer. The fermentation pages explain the decision.

You don't need to make all these choices before you start. Each section of BrewClub walks you through the options at the point where they become relevant.

## About this guide

BrewClub always explains the *why*, not just the *what*. When we recommend one approach over another, we say why. When we deviate from kit instructions, we explain the reasoning. The goal is that you understand your brew well enough to make your own decisions — not just follow a checklist.

Sanitising, cleaning, and equipment recommendations appear in each section where they are needed, with enough context to understand what you are doing and why it matters. If you want to go deeper on the science behind any of it, [The Brewer's Manual](/homebrewing/the-brewers-manual) is there — but it is never required reading for BrewClub.

BrewClub is open source, like everything on this site. If something is wrong, unclear, or missing — or if you want to add your own club's sourcing and equipment recommendations — [open an issue or submit a pull request on GitHub](https://github.com/r-colvin/craftbrewer.se).
